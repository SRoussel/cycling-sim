# Cycling Race Simulator

## Project Overview
A cycling race simulator in Go that produces realistic race results for men's WorldTour and ProSeries level racing. Uses a hybrid physics-based model with abstract ability modifiers.

## Build & Test
```bash
go build ./cmd/cycling-sim/       # build binary
go test ./...                      # run all tests
go test ./internal/simulation/ -v  # verbose simulation tests
```

Requires Go 1.24+ and uses `modernc.org/sqlite` (pure Go, no CGO needed).

## Project Structure
- `main.go`, `app.go`, `interactive_*.go` — Wails desktop app backend (package main); `gui/` is the React/Vite frontend
- `cmd/cycling-sim/main.go` — CLI entry point
- `cmd/dbcheck/` — small DB inspection utility
- `internal/service/` — race loading, start-list selection, running and saving; shared by CLI and GUI (use it, don't duplicate)
- `internal/seed/` — seed data (riders from `data/riders.csv`, courses, races, stage races)
- `internal/selection/` — start-list selection per race profile
- `internal/calibrate/` — `fit-ratings`: fits rider thresholds to real results by simulation
- `internal/stagefile/` — reads Pro Cycling Manager stage files (`.cds`) installed locally, converts route profile + cobbled/gravel sectors into segments
- `internal/model/` — Domain structs (Rider, Course, Segment, Race, Result, GCStanding) with validation
- `internal/simulation/` — Core simulation engine:
  - `physics.go` — Speed-from-power solver (bisection method), drag/gravity/rolling forces, drafting
  - `fatigue.go` — W' balance (within-stage) + cumulative multi-stage fatigue
  - `peloton_*.go` — groups (`peloton_follow.go`), effort/teams (`peloton_groups.go`), steps (`peloton_segment.go`)
  - `wind.go`, `altitude.go` — wind/echelons, climb factor, altitude; `PrepareSegments` readies a course for a race
  - `tactics.go` — Breakaway logic, sprint finish resolution, daily form variance
  - `engine.go`, `runner.go` — Orchestrate road races, TTs, and stage races (runner adds progress callbacks)
  - `validation.go` — synthetic fields; `validation_real.go` — 2024 references + 2023 hold-out; `validation_speed.go` — winning speeds
- `internal/database/` — SQLite schema, migrations, CRUD operations

## Key Design Decisions
Guiding rule: express behaviour as physics or rider intent, never as hardcoded gradient/distance thresholds.

- **Ability modifiers** (1-100 scale, 70 = WT average): `effectivePower = base * (1 + (ability-70)*0.002)`, giving ±4% range. W/kg (FTP/weight) is the main performance lever. Climbing ability blends in with the climb factor (full effect on real climbs, none on the flat). `EffectivePower` is the rider's threshold on that terrain *and* the W' threshold (`thresholdIntOn`) — keep them the same, or riding "at threshold" drains/refills W' and cancels abilities.
- **Speed solver**: bisection on `0.5·ρ·CdA·(v+w)|v+w|·v + staticForce·v = P` (w = headwind; robust on descents).
- **Steps**: segments are simulated in ≤2 km steps; tactical/incident probabilities are per 5 km (`ScaleToDistance`).
- **Groups** (`peloton_follow.go`): one rule on all terrain. Pace comes from a rotating pace line of workers (riders chasing/attacking/driving; small groups share). A big group with nobody working holds a collective tempo set by its middle riders (`tempoRiders`), not its strongest, until the finale. Followers must hold it with positional draft + spendable W'; else they're dropped. Groups line out at ≥95% threshold (no deep bunch shelter). Cooperative chases of a break spend W' N/k faster (pool depth). Groups can't pass each other; riders in a group close up to its front.
- **Contact**: riders within 10 s are one road group. Breakaway state is read from the road.
- **Effort** (`riderEffort`): each mode draws on W' once over one horizon — attack: the step; committed chase / finale (last 20 km): to the line; team driving: rest of the climb. Domestiques pace at threshold until the 3 km lead-out.
- **W'**: anaerobic capacity from peak power (`wPrimeSeconds` 14 s × sprint watts, ~15–28 kJ): puncheurs and sprinters carry more than pure climbers, which decides short climbs. Depletes above FTP; recovers with Skiba (2012) τ = 546·e^(−0.01·(CP−P)) + 316 s.
- **Durability**: in-race threshold fades ~2.5%/1000 kJ (Endurance-scaled, cap 15%). Sprint power fades the same way and loses up to 10% with an emptied W' (`FatiguedSprintPower`), so lead-out riders finish behind.
- **Altitude**: elevation integrated from gradients (course low point = 0 m); air density by standard atmosphere; aerobic power by Bassett et al. 1999 (acclimatised): ~92% at 2000 m.
- **Drafting**: `DraftFactor` is a smooth curve: 0.65 on one wheel → 0.25 deep in a bunch.
- **Stage fatigue**: `cumulativeFatigue += stress·(1 − recovery/150)`, FTP × (1 − 0.004·fatigue), floor 85% (~4% over a Grand Tour), always applied to the rider's base FTP (a bug once compounded it stage on stage, ~40% by Paris). Time cuts by climbing density (waived if >20% outside).
- **Wind**: segments have headings (synthetic in seed data); headwind enters the solver; crosswind removes gutter shelter beyond a 10-rider echelon by yaw angle; roads meander ±60° per step.
- **Terrain**: `ClimbFactor` (share of power lifting weight) classifies terrain for tactics.
- **Team strategy**: a team drives when its leader's threshold pace would break the group's fastest finisher even in the draft; its domestiques then set the pace the leader can hold on the wheel at the phase intensity (`leaderTempoSpeed`: a tempo mid-race, threshold in the finale), not an all-out effort. Leaders race in the finale; until then they sit in, even during chases, while a teammate is in their group: a leader with a rival up the road is chased for by their domestiques and only takes turns alone.
- **Stage races** (`AssignCoLeaders`, `gcRivalAhead`): each team's GC leader is its best climber by W/kg (not the ability-rating average `AssignTeamRoles` uses); domestiques who are among the 10 strongest climbers in the field, or in the GC top 10, become co-leaders. A GC leader with a rival up the road who would pass them on GC has their domestiques chase it to the line (alone, they chase themselves).

## Race control (Nov 2026)
Agreed with the user: bunch behaviour from team interest, not chase rules (`chase.go`), in stage races and one-day races.
- Planned chase: a team chases a break only if it threatens what it rides for. A GC contender's team defends its leader's place, only a top-10 place (`gcPlacesProtected`): the break may gain until its best-placed rider would pass the leader on GC. A sprint team chases a stage its sprinter can contest (`sprinterCanFollow`: holds the pace of the riders fastest on each remaining segment, with one W' tank over the run-in). A favourite's team hunts the stage when it decided to this morning and a steady 85% tempo catches the break (`stageHuntMax`). Only teams whose allowance an easy bunch wouldn't respect chase; the tightest of those sets the lowest pace-line intensity that keeps the break within it at the line (projected `timeToLineAt`); only their domestiques chase, leaders/sprinters sit in.
- Place defence is affordable-only (Nov 2026): the race leader's team defends the jersey whatever it costs; teams defending places 2-10 (and, before any GC, every contender's team) join a chase only when a steady tempo (`stageHuntMax`) keeps the break within their allowance. Measured over many runs, the old rule kept Grand Tour breaks on a 3-5 min leash all race (the tightest allowance, often 9th-10th place's team vs a break rider a few minutes further down, set the chase); breakaway wins per Grand Tour 4.6 -> 5.9 (real 7.5), rising through the race (week 3: 51% of road stages). Week-long races still have too many breakaway wins (1.6 per race, real 0): the sim has no time bonuses, which in tight week-long GCs make favourites' teams want the stage.
- Stage hunting (`setHunting`): each morning a favourite's team decides whether it rides for the stage: 50% on an ordinary stage, 100% at prestige 1 (`data/stage_prestige.csv` → `stage_race_stages.prestige`, applied in place by `seed`; judged from each stage's standing before it was raced), halved per stage its leader has won, lower for a race leader with a big margin (half at 3 min). Alone this was neutral; it matters once the bugs below were fixed.
- With a break up the road and nobody chasing, the bunch rides easy (`easyIntensity` 0.70); a break with a group behind rides together at the pace it can sustain to the line (`sustainableFraction`: threshold × hours^−0.1).
- GC contenders (`gcContender`): team leaders, or riders in the GC top 20, less far behind than a break could gain today (`breakReach`). Their attacks are followed; they and favourites don't take the random breakaway roll. Only contenders react to a rival up the road (`rivalAhead`); a leader chasing a rival rides the chase himself when his teammates lose ground to the rival's group and he is faster alone (`leaderGoes`).
- Break intent (`setBreakIntent`, `breakAppetite` 0.25): before a stage, free riders (domestiques of teams that don't need them today, leaders out of contention) may want the break; they go with non-threatening moves if they can hold the wheel.
- Team driving (`drivingTeams`/`tempoDrops`): judged over the hard stretch ahead with one W' tank for the finisher.
- Bugs found by tracing full Tours (Nov 2026), each of which killed breaks or the GC: break intent ran before the road existed (no contenders: GC leaders went in breaks); `sprinterCanFollow` and `drivingTeams` gave a full W' tank per segment (sprint teams chased summit finishes; GC teams never drove a climb, so the GC compressed to half its spread); a leader an hour down chased all day for a place; one team's tight allowance dragged every interested team into the chase.
- Validation (--sets 3): race shape 59–61% (before: 44–46%): sprints ~4.9 (real 6), breakaway wins ~10 (real 9), selections ~4.8 (real 5). Tour 2024 GC 261 s (median-gap 310; before 113 s, when breaks never won), 2023 hold-out 422 s (393), gap sum 2285 s (2077). O'Connor finishes 2nd-5th in most 2024 runs (not top 10 in reality): likely ratings fitted to the old model.
- Rejected: contenders limited to the GC top 20 (gap sum 2267 vs 2186, breaks took sprint stages).
- One-day races: the same planned chase, with a favourite's team always wanting the race (has the break back `catchMarginS` before the line) and sprint teams as above. Once the race is on (not the early phase) every group behind a move races for placings; a rider dropped behind the main field (the largest road group) chases to rejoin it (`behindMainField`). The easy bunch and the break's sustainable pacing are stage-race only: in a classic the race is the result, and the front group is often the favourites (pacing it like a break cost ~700 s). Replaced the one-day rules "everyone chases on flat/rolling roads", the cobbles chase rule and the leader chase rule. --sets 3: gap sum 2285 -> 2257 s (Flanders 2024 92 -> 55, Roubaix 2024/2023 139/126 -> 122/112; worse: Strade 2024 222 -> 264, Lombardia 2023 146 -> 176). Without the rejoin rule 2445 s; without planned chases (old rules gone) 4081 s.
- Refit after these changes rejected (Nov 2026): Tour 2024 GC 261 -> 150 s but 2023 hold-out 422 -> 465 s.
- Speed: `sprinterFollows` ranks riders per segment; the ranking is cached per group and step (`fastLines`), 5x faster.

## Special cases still to replace (audit, Nov 2026)
These break the guiding rule (gradient/distance/ability thresholds instead of physics or intent). Replace one at a time and judge with `validate --sets 3`:
- `DecideAction` (tactics_model.go), kept after testing (Nov 2026):
  - GC leaders conserve on flat/rolling terrain in stage races, including the final 85% of the time. Removing it: 2468 → 2950 s (GC leaders then chase breaks and try breakaways on flat days); a deterministic version (always, final included) also tested worse. The intent is real (flat finishes are the sprinters'); only the random 85% is a wart.
  - Done (Nov 2026): the one-day flat/rolling and cobbles chase rules are replaced by race control (see above).
- Done (Nov 2026): `breakawayProbability` is intent only (aggression × W′ left; domestiques working for a leader in the group rarely go; a team's sprinter waits for the sprint) — its ~10 km-window/terrain multipliers are gone, where a move sticks is physics. Gap sum 2739 → 2413 s; Milan-San Remo got worse (107 → 144, 65 → 122 s).
- Done (Nov 2026): favourites (`RaceState.Favourites`, the riders the bunch marks) are the `favouriteCount` = 5 fastest over the course at threshold (`markFavourites`), not an ability formula with Handling ×3 on cobbles. Marking every team leader instead was tested and rejected (3849 s: the bunch rides every attack).
- `PhaseFromKm` (60 km / 20 km) kept: phases by time to the finish (final < 30 min, middle < 90 min, timed for a median pace line) were neutral on one-day races but worse on the Tour (113 → 179 s, 150 s after a refit; sum 2397 vs 2292 s).
- One-day attacks are physics (escape.go, merged Nov 2026 once teams chased for their leaders): a leader attacks when the strongest rival leader can't hold the wheel at their burst speed here and their solo time to the line beats the group's chase pace line; a group commits while its pace line holds off a threatening rival behind (at least as strong as its weakest leader). Replaced the 8%/5 km climb-attack roll. Stage-race attacks use the same rule since Nov 2026 (replaced a probability table: km windows, 0.08–0.22, ×0.7 for the jersey, ×(1+deficit/1800)). Rejected along the way: bunch-response models that stop the whole bunch following a threatening attacker's burst (3507/3574 s) — the bunch-follows rule in `FormGroups` remains the bunch's defence; the actual cause of runaway selections was leaders chasing themselves instead of their domestiques chasing for them.

## Database
SQLite with WAL mode, foreign keys enabled. Schema auto-migrates on open. Use `CYCLING_SIM_DB` env var to set path (default: `cycling_sim.db`).

## CLI Usage
```bash
./cycling-sim seed                    # load WorldTour riders + classic courses
./cycling-sim riders                  # list riders
./cycling-sim courses                 # list courses
./cycling-sim races                   # list races
./cycling-sim run <race-id> [--incidents] [--seed N]   # simulate a race
./cycling-sim run-stage-race <sr-id> [--seed N]        # simulate stage race with GC
./cycling-sim validate [-v]           # synthetic, 2024 races, cross-season 2023 races, race speeds (-v: per rider)
./cycling-sim validate --sets N [--cross-season]  # 2024 references over N seed sets (judge model changes with this); --cross-season adds 2023
                                      # both also report race shape (how stages are won: sprint / breakaway / selection / tt) averaged per race kind, and GC spread
./cycling-sim fit-ratings [--classics] [file]  # fit thresholds to 2024 refs by simulation
./cycling-sim apply-ratings           # re-apply all rating layers to the DB in place (keeps results)
./cycling-sim import-stages <dir>     # real route profiles from local PCM stage files (keeps results)
./cycling-sim import-results [dir]    # derive ratings from saved result pages
./cycling-sim results <race-id>       # view saved results
./cycling-sim gc <sr-id> <stage>      # view GC standings
```

## Data notes
- Course segments must sum to the stated distance (`fitToDistance` in seed, enforced by a test). `seed` on an existing DB adds courses/races/stage races that are missing by name (keeps results); changing existing seed data requires `clear` + `seed`.
- `data/riders.csv`: ~370 of 521 riders share templated profiles. `data/rider_llm.csv` replaces 338 of them with LLM-drafted physical profiles (specialty, weight, W/kg; confidence high/medium/low), written offline from `data/llm/draft_spec.txt` by `python3 data/llm/expand.py [--review]`. Never call an LLM inside the race loop (speed, reproducibility). Leakage caveat: an LLM's sense of a specialty is shaped by results it has seen, so the 2023 hold-out isn't fully independent of it.
- Rating layers at seed time (`seed.RatingLayers`): `riders.csv` → `rider_llm.csv` → `rider_derived.csv` (from `cycling-sim import-results`, saved pages in `data/results/<profile>/`) → `rider_overrides.csv` (curated; GC tier calibrated to 2024 Tour gaps). Never scrape FirstCycling/PCS (robots.txt / bot protection).
- Validation is by season (agreed with the user, Nov 2026): ratings describe 2024 form, so model changes are judged on 2024 races only — six 2024 classics, the 2024 Tour and Giro GC (the races `fit-ratings` fits) and Tirreno-Adriatico and Volta a Catalunya 2024 (same season, never fitted, approximate fields: `KnownSameSeasonStageRaces`). The goal is a realistic game, so race character weighs more than exact per-rider gaps: race shape is judged on averages per race kind (Grand Tours, week-long races; breakaway rates differ between them, and a given real stage needn't come out the same way), and the 2023 Tour's shape counts towards the Grand Tour average (character, not form); GC spread compares the gaps of 2nd and 10th place. One-day finishes are measured the same way: share of runs won solo (2nd 5 s+ behind), by a small group or a bunch, and the median winning margin (Nov 2026 baseline: solo in 18% of runs over the 12 classics; real 11 of 12 won solo, by 15 s to 3 min; Paris-Roubaix ends in a bunch of 15+ in ~100% of runs). Traced Paris-Roubaix 2024: the bunch is still ~130 riders at 46 km to go, so the attack rule correctly finds a solo attack slower than the chase of a fresh bunch: the race is too easy, not the attack too timid. Tested and rejected (Nov 2026): in one-day races a favourite's team drives on cobbles/climbs when its leader is faster over the rest of the course than the group's fastest finisher — solo 18% -> 23%, Roubaix 2023 bunch finishes 95% -> 20%, but the front group stayed 36-100 riders and gap errors grew (12 classics, 20 runs: 1332 -> 1764 s; Strade 2023 387 -> 657 s). Riders get better and worse between editions, so 2023 races (`--cross-season`) mix form change with model error and are informational. References can list verified non-starters (`DidNotStart`; riders who started and abandoned ride, since abandons are chance) or the real start list with each rider's team then (`StartList`, from Wikipedia); `stageRaceField` gives every check the same field. Reference riders always start.
- `data/rider_fitted.csv` (from `fit-ratings`: the 2024 Tour and Giro GC, Nov 2026; Tirreno and Catalunya 2024 are the unfitted same-season check) is a 4th layer of `FTP_scale` multipliers (≤ ±8%, `MaxChange`). Refit Nov 2026 after the follower-capacity fix: the Tour GC compressed under the new rule and the fit lowered most riders behind Pogačar by 2-7% (Tour 2024 515 -> 40 s); the Giro 2024 check was unchanged (none of the fitted riders rode it) and the classics slightly worse. The Giro shows the opposite error (gaps too large), so Tour-tier and Giro-tier ratings were inconsistent; the joint Tour + Giro fit (after the attack rule) raised the Giro mid-tier (Martínez and Hirt at the +8% cap, Tiberi +7%, Rubio +5.8%, Arensman +5.5%) and improved both unfitted races (Tirreno 276 -> 218 s, Catalunya 665 -> 592 s) with the classics unchanged. Riders at the cap hint that part of the gap is still model. Don't fit the classics: with 1-2 results per rider it overfits (the 2023 hold-out gets worse). The fitter flags riders pushed above the strongest rider's climbing W/kg — that means it is compensating for a model gap (as it did before co-leader tactics), so fix the model instead.
- Route profiles: `data/stage_sources.csv` maps the 11 seeded classics and the 21 Tour 2023 stages to stage files of the locally installed "WorldDB" PCM mod (Steam Workshop). `import-stages` writes derived segments to `data/local/course_profiles.json` (gitignored: the source files are the mod author's; never commit them or bulk-derived data) and seeding uses it when present. A classic whose route changed between reference editions gets its own course and race (`Strade Bianche 2024`: 215 km; `Il Lombardia 2023`: Como-Bergamo, 2021 file); each mapped file exists unchanged in both WorldDB 2025 and 2026 (`workshop/content/2511310/3647555648` is WorldDB 2026: 3,234 route profiles of 872 races, 2018-2026, incl. every Giro 2022-2026; `.zces` files hold each stage's 3D scene with the road network as Bézier curves). Format notes are in `internal/stagefile/stagefile.go`; distance is in 10 m units and altitude in 7.5 m units — fixed from the climb records every file carries (start/end/length/gain in metres, which match published climbs). Reading both as one unit (before Nov 2026) made every gradient and all climbing 33% too steep; no smoothing is needed with the right units. `Tour de France 2023` (`internal/seed/tdf2023.go`) is the real 2023 route — stage distances, ITT and rest days from Wikipedia, generic template segments until `import-stages` supplies the real profiles — and is the stage-race hold-out. The generic `Tour de France` remains the 2024 reference (the mod has no 2024 Tour).
- Riders from WorldDB (Nov 2026): `cycling-sim import-riders <mod dir>` reads the mod's database (`internal/pcmdb`: zlib, a block tree of column-wise tables; format notes in the package doc) and writes WorldTeam and ProTeam riders to `data/local/riders_worlddb.csv` (gitignored). `BuildRiders` then gives every rider their real age and adds the riders `riders.csv` lacks (the ProTeams: Tudor, Pinarello-Q36.5, Uno-X, Unibet, Cofidis, ...; 521 -> 922 riders), with ratings from ridge fits of PCM attributes + weight + height on the ~505 riders both have (W/kg R² 0.69, climbing 0.61, others 0.25-0.55; fits pull extremes towards the mean, so new stars are slightly conservative). PCM ratings are never copied (they blend several things; the user's caveat). Names match ignoring accents, with extra middle names, double surnames and one-letter spelling variants; a name fitting two of our riders is skipped. `seed` adds riders missing by name to an existing DB. Without the mod nothing changes.
- Co-leaders: each stage race draws a team plan once (`RaceConfig.TeamPlan`); a domestique is freed with a probability falling smoothly around 10th in the field's climbing ranking (`freedByPlan`, `planSpread`). The old hard top-10 cut swung riders ~500 s on ±1% rating changes. Judge GC changes over several seed sets: one set of 32 runs once showed 84 s for a setup that averages ~780 s. `fit-ratings` always starts from every rating layer except the fitted one (`seed.UnfittedFTPs`).
- **One-day races** (`rivalAhead`): after the early phase, a rider with another team's leader up the road (and no leader of their own ahead) races for placings — chases to the line and takes turns — instead of riding the holding tempo. One-day team leaders are the rider fastest over the course at threshold (`AssignCourseLeaders`: climbers on hilly courses, big engines on flatter ones; sprinters keep their role) — this lets a Ganna contend in Milan-San Remo instead of always working. Validation rosters are current teams, so 2023 references can pick a leader who wasn't on that team then (Liège 2023: Landa at Soudal).
- Known gaps: on real routes, top-10 gaps in hilly classics are still too large (Liège 2023 ~360 s MAE, Strade Bianche 2023 ~530 s). In the Tour 2023 hold-out Pogačar beats Vingegaard (ratings reflect 2024 form); Grand Tour GC gaps are noisy at the validation's run count. Tested and rejected (Nov 2026): raising the whole puncheur/classics tier by +0.12/+0.25 W/kg — hold-out gaps improved but 2024 gaps and real-winner rates got worse, and results were noise-dominated at 20 runs. Paris-Roubaix runs ~9% slow because of the synthetic 20 km/h wind on synthetic headings (44.9 km/h in still air); cobble resistance and cobble drafting were tested and are not the cause. Also tested and rejected (Nov 2026): a one-day leader who gets clear of rival leaders committing to the line (mirror of `rivalAhead`) — real-winner rates rose (Strade 2024 10→53%, Lombardia 2023 25→75%) but gap errors grew in 9 of 13 races (Milan-San Remo: Pogačar solo 90%, real race ends in a group). A gated version (Nov 2026: commit only if the projected time to the line at threshold beats the chase's pace line plus the gap) was neutral within noise once it required being clear of rival leaders in one's own road group (without that, leaders inside a front group of rivals committed and Milan-San Remo got worse), because leaders rarely get clear: attacks are one-step bursts. The weak point is the attack model, not commitment. Traced Nov 2026 (Catalunya 2024 stage 3, Port Ainé): favourites never attack when one rival can follow (`attackPays` needs the strongest rival leader dropped), so Pogačar rides summit finishes at threshold with ~90% of W' unused and GC spreads come out 25-60% too small (2nd place). Tested and rejected: (1) attack when it drops any rival worth dropping (favourite/contender; any rival leader in one-day races) — top gaps became realistic but 10th place doubled and sprint stages broke up (gap sum 2082 -> 3993 s); (2) the same plus riders who can't hold the burst staying together instead of following it — 4225 s. In stage races the looser rule makes leaders attack on rolling and sprint stages and early on mountain stages; the missing piece is when an attack is worth it for GC, not how the bunch responds.
